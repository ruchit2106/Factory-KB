## Former clients
- **SKP Industries** — a **ball bearing manufacturer on Mulchand Road, Surendranagar**, and a far larger operation than ours. They sent us heat-treatment work for a long stretch of years, so the relationship is old and the people are known to us. They then built **Drawell Wires Private Ltd** at **Lakhatar village, near Surendranagar**, as their own unit — doing **complete heat treatment plus the wire drawing in-house**. No work has come to us since.
- Why this kind of loss is not the same as losing a job to a competitor: nothing was won away on price or on quality. A customer with enough volume to justify its own furnaces eventually builds them, because past a certain throughput the heat treatment stops being worth buying from anyone. The work did not move to another job worker — it left the job-work market entirely. Volume is what makes a customer valuable and, past a threshold, temporary (contrast the competitor-driven mechanism in [[Business]], The market position has shifted).
- The relationship survived the commercial end of it: they were approached for technical help on our decarb problem and the visit happened, though it did not go smoothly (see [[Business]], External expertise & tools sourcing, and [[Day15]]).

## The SuperEngiTech opportunity
- **Scale marker:** they are a giant relative to us, and considerable even at Rajkot level — and they **export**. Exporting is itself the signal: it means clearing a foreign buyer's audits, quality documentation, and delivery reliability, which a loosely-run factory cannot do. So an exporting customer is a proxy for an established, systems-driven operation.
- They run **clearly separated departments** — Quality, Development, HR, and so on — each a distinct function with its own head (our contact heads Development). This is what an organisation looks like at that scale, and it's the structural difference from how this factory runs, where father and uncle cover every function between them (see [[Business]], Leadership).
- **Why the Germans don't manufacture it themselves:** cost. India has cheap labour, and Indian labour is willing to work under conditions that European regulation would not permit — Europe's mandatory human-safety protocols add real cost to production, so the same part is cheaper to make here and ship. This is the underlying economics of the whole export-jobbing chain we sit at the end of: the work arrived in Rajkot because of a labour-cost and compliance-cost gap, not because of any technical advantage.
- The director wants to hand us **their entire heat treatment**, not just the sample work we currently do. To serve it, the plan being discussed is to **set up a new unit close to Padavla** rather than running the volume through Vavdi/Bhaktinagar.
- **Not yet a go.** They have sent our heat-treated sample on to **Germany**, and the German response decides it. So the gating factor isn't our commercial terms or our capacity — it's a third party's incoming quality verdict, which we don't control and can't negotiate.
- **Why our decarb problem doesn't block this deal**: their part is a cast hydraulic manifold — it doesn't need very high strength and doesn't face severe cyclic stress. What they actually care about is the **dimensional precision of the metal**. This is the first customer on record whose acceptance criteria are orthogonal to the issue that dominates the rest of our quality risk (contrast Pelican EngiTech's 0-micron benchmark above, and [[Business]], Decarb — business impact).
- **Why the arrangement works for us**: it makes us effectively an **extended contractor of a large group** — they give their heat-treatment work *only* to us, in mass volume, at a **fixed price**. Mass volume plus a fixed rate is the opposite of the usual job-work pattern of many small lots at negotiated per-kg rates: it makes furnace utilization predictable, which is the profit lever in this business (see [[Process]], Business model).
- It is not an exclusive tie-up in the other direction — the existing customer base continues to be served alongside it. The change is one of proportion: the majority of the work would come from this group, with the pre-existing set of customers continuing around it.

## The Pioneer dispute
- Live dispute with **Siddharth Bhai** of Pioneer, running on from the earlier decarb episode on his material.
- **New complaint: bars cracking.** He has raised it hostilely, threatening a **debit note** among other things.
- **Our position:** the cracking comes from **hardening done by someone other than us** — we did not harden those bars, so the crack originates outside our process.
- Reading of him: he is **not technical himself**, so he asserts whatever he concludes; since the one decarb mistake he has been attributing every subsequent problem to us.
- **Counterweight on the record:** during the decarb episode, the structural report showed he had sent **EN-8 instead of EN-31** by mistake — his error, which we identified and pointed out.
- Open decision at leadership level: father and uncle have been discussing **whether to visit his office in person** over this (see [[Day10]]; he last came here in person over the decarb-affected coil, [[Day5]]).
- **He has now brought in physical samples of the cracked bars** — so this is no longer a verbal claim, it's evidence in hand and testable.
- **Two separate defects are in play on his material, and they must not be argued as one:**
  - **A grain microstructure defect, which is ours.** The structure report on his material showed an **unintended grain structure**, off **furnace 12** — the same furnace behind the earlier decarb on this relationship. The cause is not yet known; the operator was questioned and ruled out (see [[Metallurgy]], Grain structure defect on the Pioneer material, and [[Workers]], Post-incident worker interrogation).
  - **The cracking, which is not.** Some bars cracked and others did not. We do not quench, and bars crack on their own when **left untempered overnight after hardening** — a scheduling failure at whichever shop hardened them (see [[Metallurgy]], Delay between hardening and tempering).
- **Why he keeps giving us the work anyway**, despite carrying this much grievance and this much material: our **rates at large quantity are cheap**. The relationship is held together by price, not goodwill — which is worth knowing before conceding anything on rate to settle the dispute. (The stock he is holding can be read off the fera rounds — see [[Business]], Reading the market through the fera rounds.)
- The practical consequence for the meeting: conceding the structure defect is unavoidable and should be done plainly, while the cracking is defensible on mechanism. Arguing both as one position loses the defensible half along with the indefensible one.
- Commercially this is the pattern described in [[Business]], Decarb — business impact: one defect at the customer's end converts into a standing trust deficit that later, unrelated failures get charged against.
