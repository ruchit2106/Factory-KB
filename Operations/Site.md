## Shed & building upkeep
- **Shed clear height is a constraint on furnace design, not just a building dimension.** A pit furnace like ours needs depth below the floor; an above-floor bell/top-loading furnace needs height above it — enough for the furnace body, the retort lifted clear of the top, and the crane travel to do the lifting. Which designs the factory can ever adopt is therefore fixed by the building before any furnace is costed (see [[Furnace-Engineering]], Furnace body design).

- **Pigeons at Vavdi**: they nest in the **corner grooves of the shed** and on **top of the crane**, and the droppings and nesting material build up over the working area. A **net has been put up** to close off both spots (see [[Day12]]).
- Why this is more than housekeeping: droppings are acidic and hold moisture, so they corrode the crane structure and any bare steel they sit on; dried droppings on a beam or walkway are a slip hazard for whoever climbs up for crane maintenance; and dry nesting material sitting on top of a crane in a hot shed is loose combustible debris directly above the working area. Netting is the root fix — it removes the roosting spot rather than repeatedly cleaning up after it.

## Incoming electrical supply
- The factory's electrical boundary is the **TC — the distribution transformer**, which steps the incoming distribution voltage down to the low-voltage 3-phase supply the site runs on. Everything outward of it is the utility's; everything inward of it is the factory's own LT distribution, and every furnace panel, motor and light hangs off it. Inward of the furnace-feeding panel, the circuitry is in [[Furnace-Engineering]], Panel.
- **Because every circuit in the factory is downstream of the TC, nothing inside the premises anywhere approaches the thousands of volts on the far side of it** — the whole site stays well below 1000 V, at the 220–230 V L-N / 380–400 V L-L level (see [[Electrical-Engineering]], AC fundamentals). That is what makes the factory's own voltmeters adequate for anything measured on site: no instrument on the floor is being asked to read a voltage beyond its range.

## Crane
- The crane is the shed's own lifting equipment — the crane **procedure** it exists for (lifting the lid, lifting the coil holder out hot) is in [[Furnace-Engineering]], Unloading and loading.
- **A crane fault is looked at before anything else on the floor.** Every loading and unloading movement goes through it, so while the crane is down the whole shed stops, not one furnace.
- **There are two cranes at Vavdi, both rated 7.5 tonnes.** That is the ceiling on any single lift on the site — a furnace lid, a coil holder, or anything else going overhead.
- **Drive motor:** a **3-phase 400 V AC squirrel-cage induction motor** — 400 V being the line-to-line figure, since no neutral goes to a 3-phase machine. How the design works and why it needs no maintenance is in [[Electrical-Engineering]], Motors.
- **The motor drives through a gear box, not an open gear train** — why that is, what the oil does, and the fill and drain plugs are in [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Gear boxes.
- **The gear box casing is a specialised aluminium casting; the gears inside it are steel.** The casing is the fragile part of the assembly and the factory cannot work on it — handle it with care, and send any work on it to a shop that can actually machine or weld aluminium (see [[Manufacturing-Processes]], Owning a casting).
- **Where they came from:** the Vavdi cranes are the **new cranes**, bought from **local crane manufacturers** — who assemble a crane from bought-in sub-assemblies rather than making it end to end, the gear box here being **SEW EuroDrive**. Why local, and who to call for a fault, is in [[Business]], External expertise & tools sourcing.
- **19-09-2026 — Vavdi crane failure (see [[Day19]]).** The crane was stripped down and the fault was in the gear box, at the joint between gear box and motor, needing a screw hole filled. The route taken was repair rather than replacement — a whole new gear box costs far more than getting the existing casing worked on (see [[Business]], External expertise & tools sourcing).
