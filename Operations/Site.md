## Shed & building upkeep
- **Shed clear height is a constraint on furnace design, not just a building dimension.** A pit furnace like ours needs depth below the floor; an above-floor bell/top-loading furnace needs height above it — enough for the furnace body, the retort lifted clear of the top, and the crane travel to do the lifting — or else a pit of its own to be sunk into. Which designs the factory can ever adopt is therefore fixed by the building before any furnace is costed (see [[Furnace-Engineering]], Furnace body design).

- **Pigeons at Vavdi**: they nest in the **corner grooves of the shed** and on **top of the crane**, and the droppings and nesting material build up over the working area. A **net has been put up** to close off both spots (see [[Day12]]).
- Why this is more than housekeeping: droppings are acidic and hold moisture, so they corrode the crane structure and any bare steel they sit on; dried droppings on a beam or walkway are a slip hazard for whoever climbs up for crane maintenance; and dry nesting material sitting on top of a crane in a hot shed is loose combustible debris directly above the working area. Netting is the root fix — it removes the roosting spot rather than repeatedly cleaning up after it.

## Incoming electrical supply
- The **TC — the distribution transformer** — steps the incoming distribution voltage down to the low-voltage 3-phase supply the site runs on. **The ownership boundary is the PGVCL meter, not the TC:** the main lines, the TC, anything between the TC and the meter (the GEB box at Micro Processors) and the meter itself are the utility's; everything outward of the meter is ours, and any fault there is our responsibility. Every furnace panel, motor and light hangs off that side. Inward of the furnace-feeding panel, the circuitry is in [[Furnace-Engineering]], Panel.
- **Because every circuit in the factory is downstream of the TC, nothing inside the premises anywhere approaches the thousands of volts on the far side of it** — the whole site stays well below 1000 V, at the 220–230 V L-N / 380–400 V L-L level (see [[Electrical-Engineering]], AC fundamentals). That is what makes the factory's own voltmeters adequate for anything measured on site: no instrument on the floor is being asked to read a voltage beyond its range.
- **Vavdi runs on 1 TC; Bhaktinagar runs on 2**, one per company — Bhaktinagar is divided between its two companies the same way Vavdi is divided between Alloy and Bright (companies in [[Business]], Our own companies / plots).
  - **Micro Processors' TC** stands **outside**, and feeds only Micro Processors: the office's shed — **furnaces 11 and 12, their vacuum pump, and the office**. It also supplies a **closed godown** next door.
  - **Micro Heat Treaters and Engineers' TC** stands **at the back**, where loading and unloading comes in for Micro Heat Treaters. It feeds **furnace 8 and the crane there** (the Micro Heat Treaters crane), and also supplies our **neighbour (wholesalers)**.
- **Only Micro Processors' supply has a GEB box** — a small box between the TC and the factory's meter that acts as an intermediate fuse, so that supply can be shut off there instead of shutting the whole transformer down. Every other supply of ours runs **directly from the TC to the factory's meter**.
- **History: Bhaktinagar used to share its TCs with houses.** A few houses of **Udyognagar**, the small locality behind the Bhaktinagar factory, ran on TCs common to our factories, and whenever the factory started it dimmed the lights in those houses (why, in [[Electrical-Engineering]], Distribution). GEB was asked to make a separate TC for us, and did.
- **24-09-2026 — overvoltage on the Micro Processors supply (see [[Day20]]).** Measured on our own multimeter from outside the GEB meters inside Micro Processors, the L-L voltage across two of the phases read **720 V**, against a normal **380–410 V**. Traced back, the higher L-L voltage started at the **output of the GEB box**. PGVCL's crew found a **wire shorting inside the GEB box**, stopped the TC and fixed the wiring by hand. They were going to fit **200 A fuses**, too low for our load; higher-rated fuses were fitted within days (how that was got: [[Business]], Electricity utility — PGVCL (GEB)); fuse ratings in [[Electrical-Engineering]], Protection: fuse vs MCB.

## Crane
- The crane is the shed's own lifting equipment — the crane **procedure** it exists for (lifting the lid, lifting the coil holder out hot) is in [[Furnace-Engineering]], Unloading and loading.
- **A crane fault is looked at before anything else on the floor.** Every loading and unloading movement goes through it, so while the crane is down the whole shed stops, not one furnace.
- **Two cranes at Vavdi, one per plot: Alloy's is 7.5 tonnes, Bright Corporation's the smaller one at 2 tonnes.** The rating is the ceiling on any single lift that crane makes — a furnace lid, a coil holder, or anything else going overhead.
- **Two cranes at Bhaktinagar as well**, one per company — Micro Processors and Micro Heat Treaters and Engineers.
- **Each Vavdi crane runs on 4 motors, one per motion:**
  - **Up/down** — the **main, biggest motor**, with a visibly big gear box. It powers the **drum** that winds and unwinds the crane rope, and it is the one that actually carries the rated load.
  - **Left/right (east–west)** — one motor powering the wheels of the carriage that runs across the crane body at the top.
  - **North/south** — **two motors**, traversing the whole crane along the shed's channel. The crane runs on **2 wheels on each side**, and each of these motors powers **one wheel**.
- **The crane's power is shut off before any repair work on it.**
- **Moving a crane that has stopped:** it can still be towed along the channel by striking the **other (unpowered) wheel** with a rod, displacing it a little at a time, to bring it to a spot where the workers can work on it easily.
- **The Alloy crane's main power is at the main panel**, along with the furnace control.
- **Drive motor:** a **3-phase 400 V AC squirrel-cage induction motor** — 400 V being the line-to-line figure, since no neutral goes to a 3-phase machine — made by **SEW EuroDrive**. It is a **brake motor** — 3 power inlets on top for the rotation and a separate 3 for the **rubber brake** that stops the shaft. The body is an aluminium casting, with a shaft-driven fan and cooling fins. How the design works, and why a crane motor carries a brake, are in [[Electrical-Engineering]], Motors.
- **The motor drives through an SEW EuroDrive gear box, not an open gear train** — why that is, what the oil does, and the fill and drain plugs are in [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Gear boxes.
- **The gear box casing is a specialised aluminium casting; the gears inside it are steel.** The casing is the fragile part and the factory cannot work on it — handle it with care, and send any work on it to a shop that can actually machine or weld aluminium (why: [[Manufacturing-Processes]], Owning a casting).
- **Where they came from:** the Vavdi cranes are the **new cranes**, bought from **local crane manufacturers**, who assemble a crane from bought-in sub-assemblies rather than making it end to end. Why local, and who to call for a fault, is in [[Business]], External expertise & tools sourcing.
- **19-09-2026 — Vavdi crane failure (see [[Day19]]).** The crane was stripped down and the fault was in the gear box, at the joint between gear box and motor, needing a screw hole filled. Repaired rather than replaced — a whole new gear box costs far more than getting the existing casing worked on (see [[Business]], External expertise & tools sourcing).
- **24-09-2026 — the gear box repair, completed (see [[Day20]]).** The repaired gear box is on the **Alloy** crane. The broken screw hole was built back up by **aluminium casting** — molten aluminium added over the hole — at an outside shop, which disassembled the box to work on it. Only the outer cast needed work; gears, internals and bearings were untouched. **On reassembly the shaft would not go back in** — the company fit could not be matched (see [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Opening a machine) — so the shaft was **turned down slightly** by Bhavesh Bhai (see [[Business]], External expertise & tools sourcing). The shaft had been working perfectly before; the lesson taken is to avoid reassembly wherever it can be avoided. The box was then cleaned in diesel, the bearings greased by hand and the box filled with **generic gear oil** last, through the oil inlet hole, filled until every gear tooth contact is covered (procedure in [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Cleaning and relubricating a gear box). **The worker washed it with water first**, which should not have been done — the water was removed completely before the diesel, but it is a mistake not to repeat.
