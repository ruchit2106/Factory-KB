1st Sept 2026

- We started actually doing the work of even gas leaking via a ring at bottom

- We took a metal pipe which was straight with length closer to the furnace diameter

- It had a inlet on both sides.

- First at closed space intervals we made extremely tiny holes evenly spaced in the pipe with 
mini drill

- After finishing that we bent the pipe roughly manually. Which lead us to approx closer ring

- The furnace inlet of the gas delivering line is made of SS. 

- We measured the Ring's Diameter and With the help of bigger hand held drill(in our factory) we expanded the already existing hole in SS pipe for gas

- SS is strong not simple as Aluminium. So we gradually increased the hole size. A good 
learning of in general drilling a hole in any hard surface. Those few steps are below

- First THe final Diameter of the hole in our destination will be slightly more than the diameter
of the drill piece (because it rotates and makes extra space).

- Secondly, when drilling in extreme hard metals (like in our case SS), dont directly drill the direct diameter,
that would be too ineffecient. Rather first use a smaller diameter drill piece to make smaller hole, than bigger than bigger 
and atlast our final required diameter piece. (We took 3 steps) 

- Since in drilling metals both the drill peice and the Final place to be holed is metal
it causes lot of friction causing lot of heat. The temperature raises easily above 500c which can be
clearly seen with the drill peice being red hot and smoke appearing. So to protect the drill piece, regularly splash 
water on top of the working drill to protect it, or take breaks and dip in water

- Drill Piece for piercing Wall has a tip with Carbide, use only for Wall and not for drilling metal

- After successful drilling the SS pipe we inserted our ring pipe (Tried temporarily without welding) as 
the diamters were too close so we though it was perfect fit. For the other end we pinched and bent its mouth
downwards thus completely closing the other ended mouth. 

- Later we did actual test, Furnace opened, we started LPG supply and lit fire close to SS-Ring junction
from safe distance with a long distant pipe soaked in diesel cloth. It showed a flame at the place of the junction, showing there is leak

- We then concluded either the Ring inserted inside is broken somewhere or the junction is not tight enough. Anyway we need welding

- We welded the junction properly. Now did the test again 

- No flame visible in junction. And then moved the fire across the entire diameter of 
ring, it lit flame all across our orriface openings. Like a series of Diwali Diyas. The 
flame size also showed the diamter of hole openings. Main thing is the last hole should be lit, since 
its the most far apart from origin

- The Ring itself is metal, sitting well close to the Heating element. Which means it can short it if touched.
Even though the Ring's diameters is shorter than the Furnace Diameter (Where Heating Element is kept).
Even a simple movement via loading and unloading the coil holder will contact it. So we decided
to firmly locate in place it via U-Pipe Clamp. So Clamps top down (tightened it with Furnace Bottom). Note that
the Clamps should be tight with the holding location BUT the clamp itself should have space
so the ring underneath it can move slightly, giving clearance for metal expansion since ring tolerates the Furnace Temperature itself

- All this Ring Installation is Done on Furnace 9 (Vavdi)

- Be careful on one thing - While welding and furnace related repairing things can be done 
climbing down the furnace and doing hands on there. For checking the LPG flame like we did,
no one should be there as while we are testing LPG keeps accumulating and a slowest flame 
can result explosivness for worker
