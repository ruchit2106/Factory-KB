# Day 18 — 16-09-2026

- **A second, different gas-distribution experiment, on furnace 10 (Vavdi)** — not a copy of the furnace 9 ring. Instead of a perforated ring sitting inside the chamber, **two more real holes were cut into the furnace, each with its own proper NRV fitting**, taking furnace 10 from 1 gas inlet to **3**. The gas source, meter and pipe run are unchanged — only the way the line enters the furnace is different. Promoted to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Gas distribution trial 2.
- **How it is plumbed:** the main pipe hits a **T junction** making two, and one of those offsets hits **another T junction**, making three inlets in all — a **parallel connection**, electrically speaking.
- **Why it is the better design of the two:** every inlet keeps **its own NRV**, so reverse-flow protection covers all three; and **nothing is added inside the chamber**, so the ring's shorting risk against the heating elements simply doesn't exist here.
- **The gas system maps onto an electrical circuit** — **pressure = voltage, gas flow = current, hole size = resistance** (inversely; a wider hole is less resistance). The three inlets being parallel means they all see the **same pressure**, bar a slight drop from the pipe's own resistance. Since the **hole widths differ**, the branches differ in resistance and each carries a **different flow** — like parallel resistors of different values off one voltage. So hole size is the only thing setting how gas splits between the three.
- **Flame test, same method as the ring:** a long **diesel-soaked cloth** held close to the face of a hole gives a constant flame. **The flame's length/height reads the gas flow** through that hole — and with pressure common to all three, flow reads hole size.
