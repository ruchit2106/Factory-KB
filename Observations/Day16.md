# Day 16 — 30-08-2026

- Visited Vavdi.
- **The fan needs its own connection made on top of the furnace lid, on every furnace** — where the oxymeter and solenoid valve connections exist only on 9 and 12. Added to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Unloading and loading, step 4 of closing up.
- **Saw the gas inlet from inside the furnace** — a **single hole** at the bottom, the one point where the whole gas charge enters. From outside, the NRV is visible on the pipe where it meets the furnace. Added to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Gas distribution topology.
- **The NRV does not stop the vacuum stroke, because the suction runs in its open direction** — vacuum pulls the gas line toward the furnace, which is the way the valve is built to pass. So the suction carries back past the inlet and the flow meter to the LPG and nitrogen bottles, and what stops it there is the flow meter's valve and the bottle's own valve. The rubber delivery pipes hold at the vacuum currently used — a harder vacuum, or a smaller-bore pipe, and they collapse outright. Added to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Spring check NRV.
- **A heating element burned through**, caught by the **ammeter in the panel not rising** — no current, so the circuit was open. This is **rare** and needs more than one parameter lining up. One of them is **too much oil on the coil surface**: with no pot or bell, the oil is exposed straight to the element, so an unusually oily batch can contaminate it. The field repair is to pinch the break and join the ends, and the joint welds itself once the furnace comes up to temperature; a proper weld is the better fix. Promoted to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Heating, Element burnout.
- **Pressure gauge scale on all 4 furnaces:** 0 = atmospheric, -1 = full vacuum, +1 = full pressure, neither extreme used. Workers write it on the sheet with the decimal dropped — **-786 means -0.786, +30 means +0.30**. Promoted to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Pressure measurement.
- **The real argument for a thyristor is heating-element life, not power saving.** A contactor puts full power into the element in a fraction of a second and shocks it every cycle; a thyristor brings it up gradually. Added to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Contactors vs thyristors.
- **Advantage of the top bell furnace: it carries its heat.** Heat traps in the top of the bell, so moving the bell to the next furnace starts that charge from a higher baseline than a pit furnace can.
- **A third furnace category — horizontal (sideways), front-loading.** The body lies on its side and the charge goes in from the front. Considered the best of the three, collecting the advantages of both pit and bell; the cost is loading and unloading sideways rather than lifting vertically by crane. Both added to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Furnace body design.
- **New decarb theory: the LPG is not circulating and never reaches the material** — a distribution problem, not a quantity problem. **The idea: a perforated circular pipe at the bottom of the furnace**, holes throughout, so gas leaves at many points instead of one — like drip irrigation on a farm. No evidence yet that maldistribution is the cause. Added to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Decarb and Ideas.
