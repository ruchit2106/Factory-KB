# Day 17 — 01-09-2026

- **Built and fitted the perforated gas distribution ring on furnace 9 (Vavdi)** — the Day 16 idea, now actually made. A straight metal pipe about the furnace diameter long, open at both ends, drilled with **extremely tiny evenly spaced holes** using a mini drill, then **bent into a rough ring by hand**. Moved out of [[Furnace-Engineering]], Ideas into its own section, Gas distribution ring.
- **Fitting it:** the furnace's gas inlet pipe is **SS**, so the existing hole in it was measured against the ring and **enlarged with a bigger hand-held drill**. The far end of the ring was **pinched and bent downwards** to close it, forcing all gas out through the drilled holes.
- **Drilling hard metal — the technique learnt.** The finished hole comes out slightly wider than the bit, because the bit rotates and cuts extra clearance. In SS, don't drill the final diameter directly — step up through progressively larger bits (3 steps here). Friction takes the bit **past 500°C**, visibly red hot and smoking, so splash water on it while running or dip it at intervals. SS is nothing like aluminium. **A wall-piercing bit has a carbide tip and is for masonry only — never for metal.** Promoted to [[Manufacturing-Processes]], Drilling hard metal.
- **The push fit failed the first test.** The ring was fitted without welding because the diameters were so close it looked like a perfect fit. Lighting a flame at the SS-to-ring junction showed a flame standing there — a leak. Either the ring was broken somewhere or the junction wasn't tight. **Welded the junction properly**, and the retest showed no flame at the junction.
- **The flame test, and what it proves.** Furnace opened, LPG on, flame introduced from a safe distance on a long pipe with a **diesel-soaked cloth**. Run around the full ring, **every orifice lit — a row of Diwali diyas**, with the flame size at each hole reading that hole's diameter. **The last hole is the one that matters**, being farthest from the inlet: if it lights, gas is reaching the whole ring. Added to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Gas distribution ring.
- **Securing the ring — and why not rigidly.** The ring is metal sitting close to the heating elements, so contact **shorts the element**; its diameter is smaller than the furnace's, but loading and unloading the coil holder alone is enough movement to bring it into contact. Fixed with **U-pipe clamps**, clamped top-down and tightened to the furnace bottom. **The clamp is tight to its mounting but deliberately loose around the ring** — it has to slide slightly, since it runs at full furnace temperature and expands. Constrain position, not expansion. Added to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Gas distribution ring.
- ⚠️ **Safety rule learnt: nobody at the furnace during the flame test.** Welding and normal furnace repair are done climbing down into the furnace, hands-on, and that is fine. During an LPG flame test it is not — gas keeps accumulating while the test runs, and the slowest flame near a worker turns explosive. Repair means a person can be inside; gas testing means nobody can. Added to [[Furnace-Engineering]], Gas distribution ring.
- The test proves gas now reaches every hole. It does not prove the decarb theory — that needs readings off treated material.
