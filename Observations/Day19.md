# Day 19 — 19-09-2026

- **The Vavdi crane motor went down, and it was dealt with before anything else.** A crane fault stops every loading and unloading movement in the shed, so it outranks any single-furnace problem. Promoted to [[Site]], Crane.
- **Disassembled it and found the fault was not in the motor at all** — it was in the **gear box**, at the **joint between the gear box and the motor**, where a screw hole had to be filled.
- **Alloy's crane at Vavdi is 7.5 tonnes** (Bright Corporation's is 2 tonnes — see [[Day20]]). Promoted to [[Site]], Crane.
- **The motor:** 3-phase **400 V** AC **squirrel-cage induction motor**. The 400 V is the **L-L** rating — a 3-phase motor is always rated by its line-to-line voltage, because no neutral runs to it. Nearly all industrial crane motors in Rajkot are this same type, and it is the most-used industrial motor design generally.
- **Learnt how a squirrel-cage motor is built:** **stator** outside (fixed, fed the 3 phases), **rotor** inside, and a **fan** on the shaft that cools the frame while it runs. The 3-phase stator currents produce a rotating magnetic field with no switching device involved, and the rotor — a shorted cage with nothing wired to it — is dragged around behind that field. Nothing on the rotor wears, which is the whole reason the design dominates industry.
- **Why cranes use a gear box and not a plain open gear train** (promoted to [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Gear boxes)**.** A gear box is the gear train **plus the whole thing submerged in oil** — every gear, shaft and bearing inside is oil-dipped. The oil takes away the heat and friction at the tooth faces, so the gears last much longer. Opened up, the train reads as: **incoming shaft from the motor → a smaller gear → the final output gear**.
- **General rule of mechanical engineering, seen again inside the gear box:** anything that rotates against a fixed part sits on **ball bearings**. Promoted to [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Bearings.
- **The casing is a specialised aluminium casting; the gears inside are metal.** Aluminium casting is the part the factory cannot touch — filling that screw hole is not in-house work, so the casing gets handled with care and the job goes out.
- **Who was called, and why them:** the **local crane manufacturer** the crane was bought from — not SEW. Our new cranes (the **Vavdi** plots, Alloy and Bright Corporation, are the newer ones) come from **local crane makers, chosen for fast response**. These local brands **assemble** a crane out of sub-assemblies bought from elsewhere — the gear box being **SEW EuroDrive** — so the assembler is the one who owns the whole machine, and they get contacted first. That is the general rule for most equipment here: for a repair, contact whoever you bought it from. Promoted to [[Business]], External expertise & tools sourcing.
- **Repair first, replace second** — an entire new gear box costs much more than working on the existing one. Next step is to take the gear box to **local welding / machining shops** and have them say what can be done to it.

- **Electricity basics for this industry.** Promoted to [[Electrical-Engineering]] — a new file, since electrical is a discipline of its own here alongside metallurgy and mechanical, not a furnace topic.
  - **Anything AC is quoted as its RMS value, never its peak** — every nameplate and every meter reading. RMS is the DC-equivalent value, so it is the number power works out from.
  - **A 3-phase supply carries two voltages:** **L-N** (single phase to neutral) and **L-L** (phase to phase). Saying "voltage" without saying which is ambiguous by 1.73×.
  - **The two most-used formulas:** **V(any)_peak = √2 × V(any)_rms**, true of L-N and L-L alike; and **V(L-L) = √3 × V(L-N)**, true of peak and RMS alike since it is just a ratio between two measurements. The √3 is the **120° phase displacement** — two phases are never at the same point in their cycles, so their difference never reaches twice one of them.
  - **The values to carry around:** **V(L-N)_rms = 220–230 V**, **V(L-L)_rms = 380–400 V**.
- **The TC is the factory's electrical boundary** — the **distribution transformer**, stepping the incoming supply down. Every circuit inside the premises is downstream of it, so nothing on site goes anywhere near the thousands of volts on the far side; the whole factory stays **well below 1000 V**. That is what makes the factory's own voltmeters sufficient for anything measured here — no instrument is being asked to read past its range. Promoted to [[Site]], Incoming electrical supply.
- **Gear boxes have an oil input hole and a drain plug hole** — one to fill, one to drain. Most are built this way, so the oil is a serviceable item and not a sealed filling. Promoted to [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Gear boxes.
- **General rule for AC machines, and it reads both ways:** **current flows in the stator** — either we give it or the machine produces it there — and **the rotor is the magnet that rotates**, either because we turn it or because the field turns it. Motor and generator are the same machine run in opposite directions. Promoted to [[Electrical-Engineering]], Motors.
