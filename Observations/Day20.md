# Day 20 — 24-09-2026

- **The crane gear box repair continued, and finished.** The broken screw hole was fixed at the shop opposite the one we bought a car LED light from, by **aluminium casting** — molten aluminium added on top of the broken screw hole. Welding it was not possible from our end. They disassembled the gear box there to work on it better. The work was on the **outer cast only** — gears, internals and ball bearings were not touched. Promoted to [[Site]], Crane, and [[Manufacturing-Processes]], Owning a casting.
- **A gear box transfers power outward: motor → gear box → final place.** Mostly gear boxes run outward only, since the motor's own input is electrical. Promoted to [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Gear boxes.
- **Reassembly: we can never match the company fitting when opening complex machine parts** — especially German brands. The shaft would not go back in, so it had to be **turned (trimmed)** a little. The shaft had been working perfectly before; the reassembly taught us to avoid reassembly. Promoted to [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Opening a machine.
- **Bhavesh Bhai** — our go-to person for machining work: turning, and grinding (big). He reduced the shaft diameter a little. Promoted to [[Business]], External expertise & tools sourcing.
- **Cleaning:** kerosene is best but a very harsh, ruthless cleaner, so diesel is second best. Soak it in, then brush and completely scrape the metal internals to remove dirt and impurities — like dry cleaning. Promoted to [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Cleaning and relubricating a gear box.
- **The worker used water to clean off the dirt — which should not have been done.** Water has to be completely removed from the gear box / machine part afterwards, because diesel and water don't mix, and any minute water residue can rust the internal gears in the long term. Avoid water at all costs. The water was removed this time; logged because it is a mistake that should not happen again.
- **Diesel cleaning removes all the pre-existing grease and oil**, so the gear box now makes a slight noise as the ball bearings rotate — the lubricant is gone.
- **Bought generic gear box oil (for the gears) and grease (for the ball bearings).** The worker applied the grease by hand, all around every ball bearing. The gear oil is filled adequately when every contact of the gear teeth is covered. After assembly, the **gear oil was poured in last**, through the gear box's oil inlet hole.
- **The crane motor, explored further:** the 3-phase squirrel-cage induction motor is also **SEW EuroDrive**. It has **3 power inlets on top for rotation** and **3 separate power inlets for the brakes** — these motors have rubber brakes that stop the rotating shaft. Promoted to [[Site]], Crane, and [[Electrical-Engineering]], Motors.
- **Motor bodies are aluminium castings** (like the gear box) — light weight, and fast heat dissipation. The motor has a **fan** driven off the main shaft, and **fins** to increase the surface area cooling the body. Promoted to [[Electrical-Engineering]], Motors.

- **Rule: any oil seal, or rubber seal in general, that sits inside the furnace exposed to high temperature needs water cooling.** Promoted to [[Mechanical-Engineering]], Rubber seals near heat need water cooling.

- **Back at Bhaktinagar — an electrical issue.**
- **Bhaktinagar is split across 2 TCs, like Vavdi is split between Alloy and Bright; all of Vavdi runs on 1.** The office's shed is **Micro Processors**, powered from a TC outside that feeds only Micro Processors — furnaces 11/12, their vacuum pump and the office — plus a closed godown. **Furnace 8 and the crane there** are **Micro Heat Treaters and Engineers**, powered from a different TC at the back (where loading/unloading comes in for Micro Heat Treaters), which also supplies our neighbour (wholesalers). Promoted to [[Site]], Incoming electrical supply, and [[Business]], Our own companies / plots.
- **TCs are step-down transformers: the 11 kV line down to 220 V.** On the commercial side 4–5 societies run on one TC, depending on the number of houses; for industries, depending on the heavy power, a TC is shared by 2–3 factories. Promoted to [[Electrical-Engineering]], Distribution.
- **Main lines → TC → PGVCL meter** is their property up to there; whatever comes outward of the meter is ours, and any fault there is our responsibility. The electric meters are Government (GEB) sealed — never tamper with them. Promoted to [[Site]], Incoming electrical supply, and [[Business]], Electricity utility — PGVCL (GEB).
- **History:** a few houses of **Udyognagar**, the small local village behind the Bhaktinagar factory, ran on TCs common to our factories, so whenever our factory started it dimmed the lights in their homes. We requested GEB (Gujarat Electricity Board) to make a separate TC for us, and they did. Now electrification is widespread and households are generally not given a common input with factories. Promoted to [[Site]], Incoming electrical supply.
- **Only Micro Processors' TC has a GEB box** — a small box acting as an intermediate fuse between the TC and our meter, so that can be shut instead of shutting the transformer down. Every other site goes directly from TC to the factory's meter.
- **The fault:** from outside the GEB meters inside Micro Processors, the L-L voltage between two of the phases was overshooting — it should be **380–410 V**, and our own multimeter read **720 V**. From the output of the GEB box onward we were receiving the higher L-L voltage, so the issue was there. We filed a complaint with GEB. Promoted to [[Site]], Incoming electrical supply.
- **GEB's crew came and found a wire shorting in the GEB box.** They stopped the TC and fixed the wiring by hand, then were going to install **200 A fuses** — not tolerable for us, they would pop for our factories any time. We asked for bigger fuses — **HRC** (High Rupturing Capacity) fuses — and the crew said they didn't have them and to take it to the GEB head.
- **Fuse vs MCB:** a fuse is one-shot, an MCB is a switch that trips. A fuse is cheaper and faster than an MCB, but when it trips a lot, the MCB wins on cost as there are no replacements to keep buying. Promoted to [[Electrical-Engineering]], Protection: fuse vs MCB.
- **Went to the PGVCL Udyognagar office** — walking distance, one street from our factory — and met the head directly: factories require higher fuses, please allow us. He agreed: 200 A temporarily, the higher fuses in the next few days — and they were fitted as promised. Promoted to [[Business]], Electricity utility — PGVCL (GEB).
- **GEB, being a Government body, is notorious for slow work** — after a complaint they come 4–5 hours later at the minimum. When dealing with them, expect delays.
